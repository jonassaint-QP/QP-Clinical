Sunday morning usually arrives with a specific kind of quiet. For many of us in the queer and neurodivergent community, that quiet is immediately filled by the hum of the Internal Auditor. It is that part of our brain that wakes up before we do, clutching a clipboard and a long list of everything we didn't quite finish during the week. It starts tallying the ADHD tax we paid on forgotten groceries or missed emails. It begins reviewing the Evidence Locker of our past social interactions, looking for any sign that we were too much or not enough. This is the Sunday Scaries in its most litigious form, and it is exhausting.

At Queer Pathways, we want to invite you to a different kind of Sunday. We want to move away from the internal courtroom and toward something we call the Somatic Reset. This isn't about fixing yourself or finally becoming the optimized version of a human that capitalism demands. Instead, it is about Radical Acceptance. It is about realizing that your body is not a broken machine that needs a mechanic. Your body is a landscape, and you are simply the one learning how to live within it.

## The Machine vs. The Landscape

Most of us were raised to treat our bodies like a 2004 Honda Civic that keeps making a weird rattling noise. We think if we just find the right therapist, the right medication, or the right productivity hack, we can finally stop the rattling and start running smoothly. When our executive dysfunction kicks in or our nervous system hits a state of cold dread, we feel like we have failed at maintenance. We look at our spicy brains and think something is fundamentally wrong with the wiring.

Radical Acceptance asks us to shift that perspective. Imagine instead that your internal experience is a landscape, like the rolling hills of Western Pennsylvania. A landscape isn't broken because it's raining. It isn't a failure because there is a steep cliff or a dense fog. The rain, the fog, and the cliffs are just features of the terrain. When we stop trying to fix the weather and start learning how to navigate it, we become what we call a Better Captain.

A Better Captain doesn't scream at the waves for being high. A Better Captain looks at the waves, acknowledges their height, and adjusts the sails. This Sunday, we are going to practice being that Captain. We are going to stop litigating why the storm is happening and start steering the ship with a bit more grace and a lot more self-compassion.

## Alex and the Sunday Auditor

Let's look at Alex. Alex is a high-achieving queer professional with a beautifully neurodivergent brain. Last night, Alex went to one of their favorite play spaces. They had a great time, felt connected, and enjoyed the sensory richness of the environment. But this morning, Alex woke up with a massive Vulnerability Hangover. The Auditor is currently in full session, arguing that Alex was probably too loud, that their neurodivergence was too obvious, and that everyone in that play space is secretly judging them.

Alex's Smoke Detector of the Brain, the amygdala, is firing off false alarms. Their body feels tight. There is a hum of overwhelm vibrating in their chest. Their Internal Working Model, that subconscious rulebook they developed years ago to stay safe, is telling them to hide. The Auditor is using the Evidence Locker of past rejection to justify this current state of shame.

In the old way of doing things, Alex would spend the whole day trying to argue back. They would try to prove the Auditor wrong. But arguing with the Auditor just keeps the courtroom in session. Radical Acceptance offers Alex a different path. It's the Pittsburgh vs. Cleveland metaphor. If Alex wants to be in a state of calm (Cleveland) but finds themselves in a state of high anxiety (Pittsburgh), fighting the fact that they are in Pittsburgh doesn't get them to Cleveland. It just makes them a very angry person in Pittsburgh. Radical Acceptance is Alex saying, *Right now, I am in Pittsburgh. It is loud here, and I feel anxious. This is where I am starting from.*

## Tracking the Body Dashboard

To start the reset, we look at the Body Dashboard. This is where we name the felt-sense cues without judgment. Instead of saying *I am failing at being calm,* we say *I notice a cold sensation in my stomach.* Instead of saying *I am too sensitive,* we say *I hear the hum of overwhelm in my ears.*

When we Master the Pause, we give ourselves a few seconds to just witness these sensations. We aren't trying to make them go away. We are just noticing them. It's like looking at the gauges on a dashboard. If the oil light is on, you don't smash the light with a hammer. You acknowledge that the engine is hot. You offer the body a bit of cooling breath. You move with intention, not for performance, but for presence.

This somatic tracking helps the nervous system learn that these sensations are safe. They are just data. They are features of the landscape. As Chris Stapleton sang in his song *Traveller*, we are all just travellers on this road. We don't always know where the road leads, but we can decide how we sit in the driver's seat. We can choose to be a Captain who treats the crew, our various internal parts, with dignity rather than disdain.

## The Point of Performance

For those of us with ADHD, the struggle often hits at what Russell Barkley calls the point of performance. We know what we should do, but the bridge between knowing and doing is out. On Sundays, this often looks like a long list of chores that feel physically impossible to start. The Auditor calls this laziness. Radical Acceptance calls this executive dysfunction.

When we accept the executive dysfunction as a feature of our neurobiology, the shame begins to dissolve. We stop trying to build a better brain and start building better external scaffolding. We acknowledge that our spoons are limited. We decide that instead of doing everything perfectly, we will do one small thing with presence. This is the shift from internal litigation to self-advocacy. This is how we begin to weave new threads of belonging within our own skin.

## A Guided Somatic Practice for Your Sunday

If you are feeling the weight of the Auditor right now, I invite you to try a simple somatic reset. You don't need to change your clothes or clear your schedule. You just need to be where you are.

First, notice the contact points between your body and the surface beneath you. Whether you are sitting in a chair or lying on your couch, feel the gravity. Notice that the earth is holding you up without you having to ask. This is the first step of Radical Acceptance: accepting that you occupy space.

Next, scan your body for any areas of tightness. Don't try to relax them. Just name them. *There is tension in my jaw. There is a weight on my shoulders.* Imagine you are a scientist observing a new planet. You are just documenting what you find. Offer those tight spots a little bit of warmth. You might even place a hand on your chest or your belly, a gesture of self-compassion that tells your Smoke Detector that there is no immediate threat.

Finally, take a slow breath in, and as you exhale, say to yourself: *Right now, this is how it is.* You aren't saying you like it. You aren't saying it will stay this way forever. You are just acknowledging the current weather in your internal landscape. This is the Radical Acceptance that leads to true change. By stopping the fight against yourself, you free up the energy you need to actually move forward.
