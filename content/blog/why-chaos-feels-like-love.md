The kind guy who texts back feels kind of boring. The guy who vanishes for three days feels like fire. What the hell is that about?

Here's the short answer. **If love felt shaky when you were young, chaos can feel like home.** Your body learned to chase. So calm can feel strange, or even scary. That's not you being broken. It's a pattern. And once you can see a pattern, you can start to change it.

You're not broken. You're just tired of the roller coaster. Let's talk about why it hooks us, and how to get off.

## Alex and the slot machine

Alex is in their late 30s and lives in Philly. Alex kept falling for the same kind of guy. First Jacob, then Tony. Both were hot and cold. A flood of sweet texts, then nothing for days. Alex spent those days staring at their phone.

Then Alex met Ben. Ben was in dental school. He texted back. He made plans and kept them. He was kind. And Alex wanted to crawl out of their own skin. Ben felt "too nice." Alex started looking for a way out.

Their therapist, Dr. Jaxon, explained it like a slot machine. When a win comes at random, you can't stop pulling the lever. Tony was a slot machine. Every now and then, he paid out. Ben was more like a paycheck. Steady and safe. But to Alex's body, steady felt like nothing was happening.

## Two drivers, one car

Dr. Jaxon drew a car with two drivers fighting over the wheel.

**The anxious driver** speeds after anyone who pulls away. "Where are you? Why didn't you text back? Did I do something?" This driver is scared of being left.

**The avoidant driver** slams the brakes when someone gets close. "This is too much. I need space. Maybe I'm not into this." This driver is scared of being trapped.

Alex had both drivers. With Tony, the anxious one was in charge. With Ben, the avoidant one grabbed the wheel. No wonder the ride felt so rough.

## The switch and the locker

The chase wasn't the only way Alex coped. Years ago, back in North Carolina, Alex drank. Alex calls alcohol the **vampire switch**. One flip, and every feeling went dark. Fear, shame, pain. All gone for a while.

Something bad happened to Alex back then. It was not Alex's fault. But the mean voice in Alex's head, the one they call the Auditor, filed it away as proof that it was. Alex calls that file **the Evidence Locker**. Every time something went wrong, the Auditor opened the locker. "See? You always do this."

That's the cruel trick of trauma. The things you did to survive get turned into reasons to blame yourself.

## The night Alex didn't drink

Alex has been sober for a while now. One night, the craving hit hard. Tony had gone quiet again. Alex wanted the switch.

So Alex **played the tape forward**. That's a recovery trick. You picture the whole night, not just the first drink. Alex pictured the second drink, and the tenth. Then the next morning. Then that night from years ago. The tape didn't end well. It never did.

So Alex did the opposite of drinking. They drove to an indoor city pool in South Philly. They jumped into the cold water and swam hard laps until they couldn't think. When they got out, the craving was smaller. Not gone. But small enough to carry.

Later, Alex saw something else. They kept saying yes to Tony just to keep him happy. Even in their kink life, which is supposed to be all about choice. That's not a full-hearted yes. That's **fawning**. Fawning is when you please someone to stay safe. Alex made a plan, said what they needed to say, and walked out of Tony's place. They ended up on the couch of their friends Will and Clint. Safe.

That's from Chapters 3, 4, and 5 of [*Pathways to Presence*](/book).

## Why this isn't a character flaw

**Your body learned what love looks like early.** If a parent was warm one day and cold the next, you learned to chase. That's how a kid stays close to the grown-up they need. It made sense then. It just doesn't fit now.

**Queer folks often learned love was risky.** Many of us grew up hiding. Love came with conditions. So it's no surprise that safe love can feel weird at first. Your body has never had much practice with it.

**Numbing isn't weakness.** Drinking, scrolling, chasing. These are ways to shut off pain that was too big. The goal isn't to shame yourself for them. The goal is to find better ways to turn the volume down.

**You can't numb just the bad stuff.** In her famous [TED talk on vulnerability](https://www.ted.com/talks/brene_brown_the_power_of_vulnerability), [Dr. Brené Brown](https://brenebrown.com/) says we can't pick which feelings to numb. When we numb pain, we numb joy too. That's the catch with the vampire switch. It doesn't just turn off the hurt. It turns off everything.

## Skill 1: Use TIPP to cool the fire

DBT is a set of skills for handling big feelings, created by [Dr. Marsha Linehan](https://www.linehaninstitute.org/). (New to it? Start with our [free DBT workbook](/resources/dbt-workbook).) **TIPP** is for when a feeling is so big you can't think. It works on your body first, so your brain can catch up.

**T is for Temperature.** Cold water calms your body fast. Splash your face with cold water, or hold an ice pack on your cheeks for 30 seconds. Alex's cold swim was a big version of this. For more body-first tools, see [Somatic Reset for Queer Minds](/blog/somatic-reset-for-queer-minds).

**I is for Intense exercise.** Move hard for a few minutes. Run up the stairs. Do jumping jacks. Swim laps. Burn off the extra fuel your body made for the fight.

**P is for Paced breathing.** Breathe in for 4 counts. Breathe out for 6. A longer breath out tells your body it's safe.

**P is for Paired muscle relaxation.** Squeeze your fists and shoulders tight while you breathe in. Let it all go as you breathe out. Notice the difference.

**A quick safety note:** If you have heart trouble or an eating disorder, check with a doctor before using cold water or hard exercise.

**Try this script:** "My craving is a 9. I don't need to fix my life right now. I just need to get it to a 6."

## Skill 2: Spot your driver

This one comes from EFT, or Emotionally Focused Therapy, created by [Dr. Sue Johnson](https://iceeft.com/). EFT looks at love as a bond, and at the patterns couples get stuck in. You can use it on your own, too.

**Notice who's driving.** Are you chasing or pulling away right now? Be honest. Most of us do both at different times.

**Name the move.** Say it plain. "I'm checking my phone every two minutes." Or, "I'm picking a fight so I can leave."

**Find the soft feeling under it.** Under chasing is usually "I'm scared you'll go." Under pulling away is usually "I'm scared I'll get stuck or hurt."

**Say the soft feeling instead.** This is the brave part. The move pushes people away. The soft feeling pulls them closer.

**Try this script:** "I noticed I wanted to bail tonight. I think I'm just scared because this feels good. Can we slow down a little?"

## A song for the thing no one says

Chapter 5 is named for "Elephant" by Jason Isbell. It's about sitting with a friend who is very sick. They both try hard not to talk about the one huge thing in the room. They laugh and drink and look away. That's what Alex did with their past for years, and the vampire switch helped them do it. It's the pick because Isbell knows this road. He got sober too. The song shows how heavy it is to not say the thing. Healing starts when you finally name the elephant.
