The holidays can sting when your family won't fully see you. You keep hoping this year will be different. And then it isn't.

Here's the short answer. **You can stop waiting for your family to change and still have a family.** You can love them and still stand on your own. And the people you choose can give you what you missed. Real healing often happens in small, everyday moments with people who show up.

You're not broken. You're just tired of waiting. Let's talk about how to let go and grow roots somewhere new.

## Lunch with Mom

Alex is in their late 30s and lives in Philly. Alex flew back to North Carolina to have lunch with their mom, Eleanor. Just the two of them.

Mom brought something in a bag. It was the patchwork coat. The one she sewed when Alex was a kid. The one the bullies made fun of. She had kept it all these years.

It was sweet. But there was a catch. Mom wanted to stay close in secret. Away from Dad. No fuss. No change.

Alex said no. Kindly, but clearly. "Mom, I'm standing in the sun. You and Dad are invited to stand with me. But I'm standing in the sun."

## The car in the rain

After lunch, Alex sat in their rental car outside the house. It was raining. Dad came out to walk Mom inside. He knew Alex was there. He didn't look at the car. Not once.

That hurt. It also made something clear. Alex couldn't grow in that soil. They had tried for 30 years. It wasn't Alex's fault the ground was bad.

This is what a family therapist named [Murray Bowen](https://www.thebowencenter.org/) called **differentiation of self**. That's a big phrase for a simple idea. It means you can be your own person and still care about your family. You don't need them to agree with you to know who you are.

## A new job for the scared kid

Back in Philly, Alex had one last session with Dr. Jaxon. Alex closed their eyes and went back to the playground. The scared seven-year-old was there. The one who grew up to be the Auditor, the mean voice that judged Alex all the time.

This time, a grown-up part of Alex showed up. A strong, loving part. Alex calls it their **Internal Daddy**. It knelt down and told the kid, "You don't have to judge me anymore. You can rest."

Then it gave the kid a new job. Not judge. **Guard.** The kid's job now is to watch the door and spot people who aren't safe. To face out, not in.

This idea comes from a kind of therapy called [IFS, or Internal Family Systems](https://ifs-institute.com/). IFS says we all have parts inside us. Even the harsh ones are trying to help. They just need a better job.

## The dinner

A few weeks later, Alex hosted dinner. Dave was there. So were Will, Clint, Sam, Christian, Chris, and Shamus. Alex's chosen family.

Someone knocked over a drink. Red juice everywhere. When Alex was a kid, that would have meant yelling. This time, everyone laughed. Someone grabbed a towel. It was fixed in a minute. Nobody was in trouble.

Then Will and Dave gave Alex a gift. A real leather vest. Earned, not bought. Later, out on the balcony, the friends shared a quiet ritual to honor Shamus.

And then they danced. Whitney Houston came on, and Alex danced with Dave and their friends in the living room. It was the dance their dad had stopped 30 years before. Alex finally got to finish it.

That's from Chapters 12 and 13 and the Epilogue of [*Pathways to Presence*](/book).

## Why this isn't a character flaw

**Letting go isn't giving up.** You can stop hoping someone will change and still wish them well. That's not cold. That's honest.

**Queer folks have always made family.** When the families we were born into shut the door, we built new ones. That's not second best. For a lot of us, it's the real thing.

**Healing happens in small moments.** A spill with no yelling. A hug instead of blame. Every one of those moments teaches your body something new. Safe love is possible.

## Skill 1: Use Radical Acceptance

DBT is a set of skills for handling big feelings, created by [Dr. Marsha Linehan](https://www.linehaninstitute.org/). (New to it? Start with our [free DBT workbook](/resources/dbt-workbook).) **Radical Acceptance** means fully accepting what is true, even when you don't like it. It doesn't mean it's okay. It means you stop fighting the facts.

Alex used this in the car. Dad didn't look. That was the truth. Fighting it would only keep Alex stuck. (Want more? Read [Radical Acceptance for ADHD and Autistic Brains](/resources/radical-acceptance).)

**Name the fact.** Say it plain. "My dad may never accept me."

**Notice the fight.** Do you keep thinking, "But he should"? That's your mind fighting the fact.

**Let your body soften.** Unclench your jaw. Drop your shoulders. Open your hands on your lap. Your body can help your mind let go.

**Feel the grief.** Acceptance often brings sadness. Let it come. Cry if you need to. Grief is love with nowhere to go.

**Turn toward what you can do.** "I can't change him. I can build a life full of people who see me."

**Try this script:** "This is how it is right now. I don't like it. I don't have to. I'm still going to live my life."

## Skill 2: Fill your marble jar

[Dr. Brené Brown](https://brenebrown.com/) studies trust. She uses a picture called the **marble jar**. Every time someone shows up for you in a small way, they add a marble. Over time, the jar fills up. That's how trust is built. Not in big moments, but in lots of tiny ones. She explains it in her talk [The Anatomy of Trust](https://brenebrown.com/videos/anatomy-trust-video/).

Alex's jar filled slowly. Dave washing the knife. Dave stepping over the broken bowl. Friends laughing at a spill. Each one was a marble.

**Notice the marbles.** Someone remembers your coffee order. Someone texts back. Someone uses your right pronouns without being told twice. Write it down.

**Notice the empty spots.** Some people never add marbles. That tells you something too.

**Add marbles of your own.** Show up for your people in small ways. Send the text. Bring the soup. Trust grows both ways.

**Trust the jar, not the hope.** When you're deciding who to let in, look at the jar. Not at who you wish someone was.

**Try this script:** "I trust you more than I used to. It's all the little things you do. Thank you."

## A song for buying dirt

Chapter 13 is named for "Buy Dirt" by Jordan Davis. In it, an older man gives some life advice. You can't buy happiness, he says, but you can spend your life on what lasts. Family, faith, love, and a little piece of land to call your own. That's what Alex finally did. They stopped trying to grow in bad soil and put down roots with the people who chose them back. It's the pick because it's about planting your life in what matters. And chosen family is some damn good dirt.
