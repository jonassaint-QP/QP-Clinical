The week does not start with a bang. It starts with an idle. For the archetype we call Justin, Monday morning is a diagnostic check. You sit in the driveway of your own consciousness, hands gripped tight on a steering wheel made of executive function and repressed tremors. You are not just a person. You have engineered yourself into the F-150 of your department. You are heavy duty. You are high utility. You are the one people call when the logistics fail or the boardroom feels like it is drifting into a ditch. You have spent decades reinforcing the frame, swapping out the soft, vulnerable components of your queer identity for an aluminum alloy that can withstand the salt and grit of corporate Pennsylvania.

This is what we call Telemetry Management. On the surface, you look like a high performer who has it all figured out. Inside, the Body Dashboard is lit up with warning lights that you have learned to ignore as background noise. Your heart rate is a sensor. Your jaw tension is a telemetry point. You do not check these things to see if you are okay. You check them to see how much more torque you can apply before the engine blows. As Russell Barkley (2012) notes, executive dysfunction is often a failure to engage the self-regulation required at the point of performance. For Justin, masking is the solution to that failure. You build a machine around the executive gaps. You become the truck so that no one notices the driver is white knuckling it through every turn.

This engineering project follows you from the boardroom into the bedroom. In the landscape of Bottomsburgh, you are a rare commodity that does not actually exist. You are sexually submissive. You want to be pinned down, unmapped, and handled with the same care you give your quarterly reports. But because you have built the F-150 body, because you project the air of the Provider and the Competent Architect, people read you as the top before they ever ask a single real question. This is the Unasked Identity. This is the communal assumption. They hand Justin the keys and tell him to drive before checking the diagnostic. They see the frame, the competence, the high-utility presentation, and appoint him the Default Top, the Presumed Provider, without inquiry. But the deeper fracture is this: Justin is so good at hauling everyone else's logistics and performing the F-150 role that he does not recognize he is baiting the community with a survival script. He thinks he is signaling desire when he is actually signaling usefulness. He thinks he is offering authentic top energy when he is really running an emergency protocol so old it feels like identity. He does not know the engine can be turned off. That topping is not desire. It is labor. It is corporate-flavored performance dragged into the bedroom. The realization that the body is actually submissive, and that the topping was never erotic congruence but output, lands as a somatic shock. It hits like cold dread in the chest, like the steering wheel suddenly going loose in your hands. You find yourself in play spaces where the tops are nowhere to be found. Then the Historical Fiction of the Verse Profile enters the record. Justin views the Verse taxonomy, Verse-top, Verse, Verse-bottom, as a collection of submissives with performance-anxiety-flavored top filters. Even when a Verse-top claims they can perform, Justin files it as a Motion for Reconsideration based on outdated evidence. Whether they topped ten years ago or last night is irrelevant. It means nothing because it is not tonight. That disbelief adds weight to the frame. Justin has to engineer the entire container, the RACK scene, the logistics, the penetration, because the Verse noise in Bottomsburgh does not feel stable enough to let the mask come off. To even get a seat at the table of RACK (Risk-Aware Consensual Kink) sex, you are forced to top. You provide the penetration you actually want to receive just to facilitate the scene. This is not erotic congruence. This is exhausting utility. This is the F-150 mask doing what it was built to do: haul, organize, compensate, perform. The submissive reality sits underneath that labor, cold and waiting, wanting less output and more surrender. This is the ultimate somatic debt. You are performing labor in your most intimate moments because the machine you built for survival has become your only recognizable identity. Justin is the F-150 because no one else is reliable enough to drive the truck.

The Auditor is the one who keeps the maintenance logs. It is the internal prosecutor that demands a return on investment for every ounce of energy you spend. It tells you that if you stop being the truck, you will become a wreck. It uses the evidence locker of your past failures, the times the ADHD brain let a deadline slip or a social cue go unread, to justify the weight of the armor. Brené Brown (2012) describes this as the heavy armor we carry to protect ourselves from the pain of being seen. For Justin, the armor is not just a metaphor. It is a felt sense of compression. It is the hum of a machine that never sleeps. You are afraid that if you turn the engine off, it will never start again.

Rule 9: Front load the Bullshit, Reward the Hero. The bullshit is the exhaustion. The bullshit is the realization that you are providing a service to everyone in your life while your own tank is bone dry. The hero is the version of you that is allowed to park the truck and step out into the rain. We see this in the acoustic ecology of Zach Bryan (2022) when he sings about wanting to be a child again or the man he was before he became a machine. That longing is the somatic signal that the frame is beginning to crack. You are not broken. You are just unmapped. You have navigated the corporate world using someone else's GPS, and it has led you to a place where you are a stranger to your own skin.

Rule 6: Seek the Leather. This is where the clinical meets the tactile. When the internal machine is failing, we look for an external skeleton. In the kink community, leather is more than an aesthetic. It is a somatic reset. It provides the compression and the sensory boundaries that the ADHD brain craves. It allows the F-150 body to rest because the leather is doing the work of holding everything together. It is a way to bridge the gap between the Service Top mask and the submissive reality. In the safety of the gear, you can finally stop monitoring the telemetry. You can finally just be the body.

The work we do at Queer Pathways is not about fixing the truck. It is about mapping the terrain. We look at the Internal Legal System of the Marginalized Heart and we ask who is actually in charge of the maintenance schedule. Is it the Auditor, driven by shame and corporate survival? Or is it a Better Captain, one who knows when to downshift and when to let the engine cool? Change is quiet and incremental. It is the small decision to notice the hum of the machine and choose a Sovereign Pause instead of another hour of labor. It is the realization that your utility is not your value.

Nothing here is broken. You have survived a world that was not built for your neurodivergence or your queerness by becoming the most reliable machine in the fleet. But the F-150 was never meant to be driven at redline for twenty years straight. It is time to pull into the shop and look at the blueprints. It is time to find the paths that lead back to your own presence.
