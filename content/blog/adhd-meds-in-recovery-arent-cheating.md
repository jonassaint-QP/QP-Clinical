You're sober and proud of it. But your ADHD is wrecking your days. Then someone says ADHD meds are "just another drug." Ugh.

Here's the short answer. **Treating your ADHD the right way is not a relapse.** For many people in recovery, the right ADHD medicine, taken as prescribed, makes life steadier. Think of it as weight in the bottom of a boat. It keeps you from tipping over. The key is working with a prescriber who knows both ADHD and recovery.

You're not broken. You're just tired of white-knuckling it. Let's talk about why this choice can be brave, not weak.

## Nobody is coming to save you

Alex is in their late 30s and lives in Philly. Things with Dave were going well. He's kind and steady. And a part of Alex wanted Dave to fix everything.

Dr. Jaxon, Alex's therapist, gently pushed back. Dave can love Alex. Dave can't be Alex's savior. The scared parts of Alex need an answer from inside. From Alex's own Wise Mind, the calm part that knows what's true.

So Alex started building a life that could hold steady, with or without a rescuer.

**Clear agreements.** Alex and Dave chose an open relationship. They made clear, honest rules together. They checked in often. The point was safety, not secrets. (More on that in [Radical Trust: Negotiation as a Love Language](/blog/radical-trust-negotiation-as-a-love-language).)

**Time in the trees.** Alex started walking in the Wissahickon. One day, they leaned their head against an old oak. For the first time in a long time, their brain went quiet. Nature can do that. It gives a busy mind a rest. Alex also felt some old church guilt start to loosen.

**Owning what they want.** Alex told Dr. Jaxon something they wanted in bed. Alex was afraid it said something about who they were. Dr. Jaxon helped them see it as a choice they get to make. Not a label stuck to them forever.

## The man box

Growing up, Alex lived by a set of rules. Some people call it the **man box**. It's a box of rules about being a "real man." Don't cry. Don't hurt. Don't need anyone.

Alex remembers a soccer game as a kid. They cut their shin and it bled. Their dad taped it up and told them to get back out there. Nobody asked if it hurt.

Years later, at 2 in the morning, Alex burned their finger on a hot tool in their workshop. Their first urge was to wrap it in electrical tape and say nothing. Just like Dad would. Instead, Alex went and woke up Dave. "I hurt myself. Can you help?"

That's a small moment. It's also huge.

## The medicine question

Alex had never treated their ADHD. They were scared to. Alex is in recovery. Wouldn't a stimulant just be another way to use?

Alex met with a psychiatrist. They talked through everything, including Alex's past with drinking. The doctor suggested a common ADHD medicine and a plan to keep a close eye on it.

Dr. Jaxon helped with the shame part. Untreated ADHD is itself a big risk for relapse. A [large 2017 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28659039/) looked at almost 3 million people with ADHD. They had fewer ER visits tied to drugs or alcohol during the months they took their ADHD medicine. Medicine, used the right way, can be **ballast**. Ballast is the weight in the bottom of a boat that keeps it upright in rough water.

Then one day, Alex dropped Dave's favorite blue bowl. It shattered. Alex froze and waited to get yelled at. Dave stepped over the pieces and hugged them. The bowl didn't matter. Alex did.

That's from Chapters 10 and 11 of [*Pathways to Presence*](/book).

## Why this isn't a character flaw

**Needing help isn't weak.** The man box says strong people go it alone. That rule hurts everyone, and it hits queer folks hard. Real strength is letting someone in.

**ADHD is a medical thing.** You wouldn't skip glasses because you're "supposed" to see better. ADHD medicine is a tool for a real brain difference.

**Recovery is about freedom, not suffering.** Sobriety was never meant to be a test of how much pain you can stand. If a safe, monitored treatment helps you stay well, that's recovery working.

**Vulnerability takes guts.** [Dr. Brené Brown](https://brenebrown.com/) found that for many men, shame comes down to one rule: don't look weak. That's the man box in one line. But in her [TED talk on vulnerability](https://www.ted.com/talks/brene_brown_the_power_of_vulnerability), she says vulnerability is where courage starts. Waking up Dave took more guts than taping the burn.

## Skill 1: Do the opposite of hiding

DBT is a set of skills for handling big feelings, created by [Dr. Marsha Linehan](https://www.linehaninstitute.org/). (New to it? Start with our [free DBT workbook](/resources/dbt-workbook).) **Opposite Action** is a DBT skill for when a feeling pushes you to do something that won't help.

Shame wants you to hide. Fear wants you to run. Opposite Action means doing the reverse, on purpose. Alex did this when they woke up Dave instead of taping their finger shut.

**Name the feeling.** "I feel ashamed." Or, "I feel scared."

**Name the urge.** What does the feeling want you to do? Hide? Lie? Go quiet?

**Check if it fits.** Ask, "Will doing this help me right now?" If not, it's time for Opposite Action.

**Do the opposite, all the way.** If shame says hide, tell someone safe. Stand up straight. Make eye contact. Use a steady voice. Half-doing it doesn't work as well.

**Try this script:** "I want to hide this. So I'm going to say it out loud. I'm scared to try ADHD meds because I'm in recovery."

## Skill 2: Prep for your meds talk

This one is an ADHD skill. ADHD brains often blank out in doctor visits. You walk in with ten questions and walk out with zero. So write it down first.

**List what's hard.** Write three ways ADHD gets in the way. Missed bills. Lost jobs. Forgetting to eat. Real examples help.

**Be honest about recovery.** Tell your prescriber about your history with drinking or drugs. It's not there to judge you. It helps them pick the safest plan.

**Bring your questions.** "Is there a longer-acting option that's harder to misuse?" "How will we check in?" "What should I watch for?"

**Bring a person, if you want.** A friend or partner can take notes. Two brains remember more than one.

**Make a safety plan.** Decide who else knows you're starting a medicine. Some people have a partner hold the bottle at first. That's not shame. That's a smart plan.

**Try this script:** "I have ADHD, and I'm in recovery from alcohol. I want to treat my ADHD safely. Can we talk about options and how we'll keep an eye on it?"

## A song for a better boat

Chapter 10 is named for "Better Boat" by Kenny Chesney. It's about storms you can't control. You can't stop the waves, so you learn to build a sturdier boat and keep going. That's what Alex did. They couldn't make life calm, so they added ballast. Medicine, clear agreements, trees, and people who show up. It's the pick because it's gentle about getting stronger. You don't have to be tougher. You just need a better boat.
